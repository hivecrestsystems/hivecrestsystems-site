**The short answer:** at list price, Google Workspace and Microsoft 365 now cost the same for a small business: $7, $14 and $22 per user a month on a yearly commitment ([Google Workspace Admin Help][g-prices]; [Microsoft licensing notice][ms-notice]). So the choice comes down to the apps your team and customers already use, how much storage each person needs, and how tightly you need to control company laptops and phones.

Many comparisons online still show Microsoft as cheaper because they were written before its price rise on July 1, 2026. A Microsoft partner that checked both vendors' pages on August 24, 2026 found the three tiers matched to the dollar ([ABT][abt]). This guide compares the plans, then covers which suits which business, email on your own domain, switching, and the security settings to turn on first. Prices are US list prices as of November 2026, before sales tax.

## At a glance

| Compared | Google Workspace | Microsoft 365 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Entry plan | Business Starter, $7 | Business Basic, $7 |
| Middle plan | Business Standard, $14 | Business Standard, $14 |
| Top small business plan | Business Plus, $22 | Business Premium, $22 |
| Paying month to month | $8.40, $16.80 and $26.40 | About 20% more, by one Microsoft partner's estimate |
| Storage on the entry plan | 30 GB per user, shared by mail and files | 1 TB of files, plus a 100 GB mailbox |
| Installed desktop apps | None; you work in the browser and mobile apps | Word, Excel, PowerPoint and Outlook, from Standard up |
| AI assistant | Gemini in every plan, limited on Starter | Basic Copilot chat in every plan; the full Copilot costs extra |
| Added in the top plan | Vault, for legal holds and records search | Device management and stronger threat protection |

Prices are per user a month, paid yearly, and each is sourced below.

### What the price tables don't show

- **Microsoft's increase reaches existing customers at renewal.** New purchases have paid the new price since July 1, 2026; existing subscriptions keep the old rate until their next renewal ([BriteCity][britecity]).
- **Paying monthly costs more on both.** Google's no-commitment price is 20% higher ([Google Workspace Admin Help][g-prices]). One Microsoft partner puts Microsoft's at about 20% higher too ([Platinum Systems][platinum]); Microsoft's own page lists only yearly prices ([Microsoft][ms-compare]).
- **Google sometimes discounts.** Google says it occasionally offers discounts in some countries ([Google][g-plans]), and one reviewer saw half-price introductory offers in October 2026 ([Lilach Bullock][bullock]).
- **Nonprofits pay less on Google:** a free nonprofit edition, and Business Standard at $3.50 a user a month ([Drag][drag]). If you're a nonprofit, ask Microsoft what it offers too.
- **Tax is extra.** In Maryland, software bought for business use carries 3% sales tax ([Comptroller of Maryland][md-tax]). See [the Maryland rules](#rules-for-maryland-businesses).

## Which one fits your business

With prices equal, choose by the files you exchange, the devices you manage and what your staff already know. Switching later means migration fees and retraining.

| If your business | Leans toward | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trades Word and Excel files daily with clients, lenders, accountants or agencies | Microsoft 365 Business Standard | Installed apps and exact file formats; in Google's apps, layouts and formulas don't always survive |
| Works mostly in a browser, on mixed devices, with a lot of real-time co-editing | Google Workspace | Simpler to run, with nothing to install |
| Needs to manage and lock down company laptops and phones | Microsoft 365 Business Premium | Device management and threat protection in one plan, for $8 a user more than Standard ([UC Today][uctoday]) |
| Wants an AI assistant in the apps without an add-on | Google Workspace Business Standard | Gemini in the apps at $14; Microsoft's full Copilot costs $18–$21 a user a month extra ([Microsoft][ms-compare]) |
| Has staff with very large mailboxes on the cheapest plan | Microsoft 365 Business Basic | A 100 GB mailbox, against 30 GB shared by mail and files |
| Sells to government or works under a prime contractor | Ask the customer first | Contracts sometimes name the platform or the security settings required |
| Already runs on one, and it works | Stay | Moving costs $15–$185 a mailbox ([MedhaCloud][medhacloud]; [itGenius][itgenius]), plus retraining |

For where an AI assistant actually saves a small team time, see [where AI and automation help a small business](/guides/ai-automation-small-business).

### What makes the yearly bill higher or lower

| Driver | Costs more | Costs less |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plan mix | Everyone on the top plan | Each person on the plan their job needs; plans can be mixed |
| Billing term | Month to month, about 20% more ([Google Workspace Admin Help][g-prices]; [Platinum Systems][platinum]) | A yearly commitment |
| Unused accounts | Licenses left on former staff | A check at every renewal |
| AI add-ons | Copilot for every user | Add-ons only for the people who use them |
| Shared addresses | A paid license for info@ and billing@ | Groups or shared mailboxes, which usually need no license |
| Extra storage | Moving up a tier for one heavy user | Archiving, or moving large files out of mail |

## Setting up email on your own domain

The job is the same on both: prove you own the domain, point mail at the new service, then add three records that stop others from faking your address. Skipping those three records now causes delivery problems, not just a security gap.

| Step | What it does | Where it's done |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Verify the domain | Proves to Google or Microsoft that you own the name | A text record at your domain registrar, the company you bought the domain from |
| 2. Create users and addresses | One license per person; info@ and similar can usually be a free group or shared mailbox | The admin console |
| 3. Point mail at the service (MX records) | Makes new mail arrive in the new mailboxes | The domain registrar |
| 4. SPF | Lists which services may send mail for your domain | A text record at the registrar |
| 5. DKIM | Adds a signature that proves a message wasn't altered | Turned on in the admin console, then a record at the registrar |
| 6. DMARC | Tells receiving servers what to do with mail that fails those checks, and sends you reports | A text record at the registrar |

Steps 1–3 get mail working. Steps 4–6 are the ones small businesses tend to skip.

### What Gmail requires of anyone sending to it

Since February 1, 2024, Google has required the following of anyone sending to Gmail addresses. Mail that fails may be marked as spam or rejected ([Google][g-senders]).

| Requirement | Applies to |
| --- | --- |
| SPF or DKIM set up for the sending domain | Everyone |
| Mail sent over an encrypted connection (TLS) | Everyone |
| Spam complaints kept below 0.3%, and ideally below 0.1% | Everyone |
| SPF, DKIM and DMARC, all three | Bulk senders: more than 5,000 messages a day to Gmail accounts |
| One-click unsubscribe on marketing mail | Bulk senders |

A ten-person business is nowhere near 5,000 a day, but its newsletter tool may count toward the limits, so setting up all three is the safe course. Every service that sends in your business's name needs covering too, such as invoicing software and your website's contact form.

Record changes usually take effect within hours, though some take longer. A new domain with a handful of users can often be sending and receiving mail the same day.

## What moving from one to the other involves

When a provider does the move, a small business typically pays $15–$185 per mailbox ([MedhaCloud][medhacloud]; [itGenius][itgenius]). For under 50 users, the transfer itself takes 1–5 days ([MedhaCloud][medhacloud]; [Iron Cove Solutions][ironcove]); with planning, a move of 25–50 users takes 1–3 weeks from start to switch-over ([Always Beyond][alwaysbeyond]). Email, calendars and contacts move well. Files and anything automated take the real effort.

### Ways to move the data

| Route | Cost | What to know |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Microsoft's built-in migration tool | Included with the subscription | Moves Google mail into Microsoft 365; its support for calendars and contacts is limited ([Always Beyond][alwaysbeyond]) |
| Basic mail-only copy (IMAP) | Free | Slow, about 1–2 GB per mailbox a day, and it can't top up with new messages later ([Tygart Media][tygart]) |
| Google's migration tool | Included from Business Standard, according to one reviewer ([SaaS CRM Review][saascrm]) | Check Google's own help pages for what it moves |
| Paid migration software (MigrationWiz) | About $12–$15 per user, and about $50 per shared drive ([Tygart Media][tygart]; [TrustedTech][trustedtech]) | Moves mail, calendars, contacts and files, and can sync changes again just before the switch |

### What providers charge to do it for you

Published prices, and what ten mailboxes would cost:

| What's covered | Published price | Ten mailboxes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mail, calendars and contacts into Microsoft 365 | $15 per user, plus a $500 setup fee ([MedhaCloud][medhacloud]) | $650 |
| Google Workspace to Microsoft 365, priced by data volume | $20–$50 per user ([Iron Cove Solutions][ironcove]) | $200–$500 |
| Mail, calendar, contacts and files | $80–$120 per user, plus a base setup fee ([Cloudhouse Technologies][cloudhouse]) | $800–$1,200, plus setup |
| Microsoft 365 to Google Workspace, with domain records and security set up | $185 per mailbox, or $497 for a fresh setup ([itGenius][itgenius]) | $1,850 |

The spread reflects what's included, especially files and security setup.

### What makes a move cost more

- **Large mailboxes:** archives over 10 GB per user slow everything down ([Cloudhouse Technologies][cloudhouse]).
- **Shared mailboxes and delegated access:** these have to be rebuilt by hand ([Cloudhouse Technologies][cloudhouse]).
- **More than one domain.**
- **Files:** shared drives are priced separately ([Tygart Media][tygart]), and Google Docs and Sheets convert to Office formats with some layout changes.
- **Anything automated:** scripts, forms and connected apps have to be rebuilt, and one firm says this work often runs two to three times over the first estimate ([Tygart Media][tygart]).

A careful move copies the mail first while people keep working, switches the mail records on a quiet evening or weekend, then runs a final sync. Keep the old accounts for at least a month before you cancel them.

## Security settings to switch on first

The most valuable setting on either platform is a second sign-in step for every user, often called two-step verification. Microsoft says that, together with blocking older sign-in methods, it stops more than 99.9% of common identity attacks ([Microsoft][ms-security]). It's included at no extra cost on both ([Google Workspace Admin Help][g-2sv]; [Microsoft][ms-security]).

### How each one handles the second step

| Setting | Google Workspace | Microsoft 365 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| On by default? | No, for most accounts; an admin must allow it, then enforce it | New accounts may start with Microsoft's baseline settings, called security defaults, switched on |
| What enforcing it does | Covers everyone or chosen groups, now or from a set date | Every user must register; admins are asked at every sign-in |
| Grace period for new users | The admin sets one, from 1 day to 6 months | 24 hours for a brand-new account; the old 14-day period was removed on July 29, 2024 |
| Stronger options | The admin can block text-message and phone-call codes, or require security keys; passkeys count | Older sign-in methods that skip the second step are blocked |
| Admin accounts | Google is enforcing it for administrators | Asked every time |

Google's details are from its admin help ([Google Workspace Admin Help][g-2sv]), and Microsoft's from its security defaults page, updated October 16, 2026 ([Microsoft][ms-security]).

### A short checklist for either platform

| Do this | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Enforce the second sign-in step for everyone, preferring an app prompt, passkey or security key over text-message codes | Text codes can be intercepted or redirected |
| Keep a separate admin account that isn't used for daily email | A hacked inbox shouldn't hand over the whole business |
| Have two admins, both inside the business | One person leaving or being locked out shouldn't stop everything |
| Remove accounts the day someone leaves | Old accounts are a risk, and unused licenses build up over time ([UC Today][uctoday]) |
| Back up mail and files with a separate service | Neither subscription is a backup, and deleted items are purged after a set period. One provider calls a separate backup a small monthly add-on ([Mainsail Data][mainsail]) |
| Require a screen lock and encryption on devices that hold company data | Laptops and phones get lost |

### When the top plan is worth it

Microsoft's Business Premium adds device management and stronger threat protection for $8 more per user than Standard ([UC Today][uctoday]). It suits a business that handles client financial, health or legal records, or one that a customer or insurer has asked about device controls. If your plan includes Microsoft's premium identity features, Microsoft recommends tailored sign-in rules in place of security defaults ([Microsoft][ms-security]). Google's Business Plus, also $8 more than its Standard plan, adds Vault for legal holds and records search ([eesel][eesel]; [Dupple][dupple]).

## Red flags when you set up or switch

The most damaging mistake is letting someone else own the admin account or the domain. Whoever holds those two things controls your email.

| Red flag | Why it matters | What to look for instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Your IT provider is the only admin, or the account is in their name | You can be locked out of your own email | You hold an admin account, and the provider is added as a second admin |
| The domain is registered in a contractor's or former employee's name | Your email and website both depend on it | The domain in your business's own registrar account |
| Licenses billed through a reseller, with a markup or lock-in nobody mentioned | Switching gets harder, and the cost is hidden | Billing direct, or a reseller agreement that states the price and the exit terms |
| No second sign-in step, because staff find it a nuisance | With older sign-in methods blocked, it stops more than 99.9% of common identity attacks ([Microsoft][ms-security]) | Enforced for everyone, admins first |
| A migration quote with no list of what moves | Files, shared mailboxes and automations are where extra costs appear | A written scope covering mail, calendars, contacts, files and shared mailboxes |
| Several people sharing one mailbox password | No record of who did what, and it breaks sign-in protection | A shared mailbox or group, with each person signing in as themselves |
| No written handover | Nobody knows what was set up | A one-page record of admins, plans, domain records, backup and renewal dates |

## Rules for Maryland businesses

This section is general information, not legal or tax advice. Check with a tax professional or an attorney, and confirm current figures with the official source, because the rules change.

| Rule | What it says | What it means for you |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sales tax on the subscription | Cloud software bought for a business's own computer systems is taxed at 3%, and the same software sold for individual use at 6%. The seller collects it ([Comptroller of Maryland][md-tax]) | Ten people on a $14 plan pay $1,680 a year, plus $50.40 in tax |
| Sales tax on setup and migration | IT services have been taxed at 3% since July 1, 2025 ([Comptroller of Maryland][md-tax]) | Expect 3% on a provider's invoice; ask how the work is classified |
| Breach notice (Personal Information Protection Act) | A business holding Maryland residents' personal information must investigate a breach. Unless misuse is unlikely, it must notify the people affected within 45 days of discovering it, after first notifying the Attorney General ([Maryland Commercial Law § 14-3504][md-breach]) | A hacked mailbox holding customer records can trigger this |
| Buy Maryland Cybersecurity Tax Credit | A Maryland business with fewer than 50 employees in the state can claim 50% of the net price of cybersecurity technology or services bought from a seller certified by the Department of Commerce, up to $50,000 a year ([Maryland Department of Commerce][md-credit]) | The Google or Microsoft subscription itself doesn't qualify; security tools or services from a certified Maryland seller can |

The credit is first come, first served, capped at $4 million a year: $3 million for technology and $1 million for services. Each seller is capped at $200,000 in credits a year, and Commerce says some have reached their 2026 cap, so confirm the seller is certified and has room before you buy. You apply online and attach the certificate to that year's Maryland income tax return; review takes about 60 days ([Maryland Department of Commerce][md-credit]).

Which tax rate applies depends on whether the software is bought solely for use in a business's computer system ([Comptroller of Maryland][md-tax]). If you're a sole proprietor mixing personal and business use, ask an accountant. For more, see [Maryland's 3% tax on IT and software services](/guides/maryland-tax-on-it-services).
