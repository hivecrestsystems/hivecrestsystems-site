**The short answer:** a professional small business website costs about $1,000–$8,000 from a freelancer and $6,000 or more from an agency. It takes 4–8 weeks to build, and $50–$500 a month to keep running.

The range is wide because "a website" can mean three pages with your own text, or fifteen pages with copywriting, photos and a booking system. This guide breaks the cost down by type of site, explains what moves the price, and covers what to budget after launch, so you can compare quotes line by line rather than by the total.

## At a glance

| Type of website | Freelancer | Agency | Monthly after launch | Timeline |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Simple site | $1,000–$8,000 | $6,000–$35,000+ | $75–$150 care plan, or a $17–$40 builder plan if you manage it yourself | 1–8 weeks |
| Site you edit yourself | $1,200–$12,000 | $8,000–$25,000 | $20–$150 hosting, plus a $75–$250 care plan | 4–8 weeks |
| Shopify store | $2,000–$10,000 | $8,000–$50,000+ | $39–$399 Shopify plan, plus $200–$500 in apps once established | 2–12 weeks |
| Custom web app or customer portal | From about $18,000 | $25,000–$140,000 | 15–25% of the build cost a year | 2–12 months |

These are build costs. Domain, hosting and platform fees come on top, as covered in [what to budget after launch](#what-to-budget-after-launch).

**In Maryland, add 3% sales tax.** Since July 1, 2025, Maryland charges 3% sales tax on custom web design, custom programming and web hosting, with no small business exemption ([Comptroller of Maryland][md-tax]). Expect to see it on quotes; [our guide to the Maryland tax](/guides/maryland-tax-on-it-services) covers what it applies to.

## What each type of site includes, and what it costs

### A simple site

One to ten pages, a contact form and a layout that works on phones.

- **Do it yourself:** about $300 in the first year on a website builder, including a domain and email ([UENI][ueni]).
- **Freelancer:** $1,000–$5,000 ([UENI][ueni]) to $1,500–$8,000 ([Jim][jim]).
- **Agency:** $6,000–$12,000 at a boutique studio; $12,000–$35,000+ at a full-service agency ([Jim][jim]).

### A site you can edit yourself

The same, built on a content management system such as WordPress or Webflow, with editing access and training.

- **Do it yourself:** WordPress costs $0–$200 for a theme, plus $5–$30 a month for hosting ([Jim][jim]).
- **Freelancer:** WordPress $1,200–$10,000; Webflow $3,500–$12,000 for a 15-page site ([Project Cost Estimator][pce-webflow]).
- **Agency:** $8,000–$25,000 with branding, copywriting and the content system included ([Project Cost Estimator][pce-webflow]).

### A Shopify store

Theme setup, products, payments, shipping, taxes and training.

- **Do it yourself:** $500–$3,000 in the first year, including a paid theme, apps and photos ([Ecosire][ecosire]).
- **Freelancer:** $2,000–$10,000 ([Ecosire][ecosire]).
- **Agency:** $8,000–$25,000 at a small agency; $25,000–$50,000+ at an established one ([Ecosire][ecosire]).

### A custom web app or customer portal

Logins, dashboards, bookings and client areas.

- **Do it yourself:** not realistic beyond simple no-code tools.
- **A simple first version:** $18,000–$36,000 at offshore rates ([TekRevol][tekrevol]).
- **Agency:** $25,000–$75,000 for an internal tool; $55,000–$140,000 for a customer portal ([TekRevol][tekrevol]).

### The hourly rates behind those prices

- **WordPress developers:** $50–$95 an hour ([WebsiteSetup][websitesetup]).
- **Shopify:** freelancers $30–$150 an hour; senior US or UK agency engineers $150–$250+ ([Clean Commit][cleancommit]).
- **Web app teams in North America:** $100–$180 an hour, blended ([TekRevol][tekrevol]).

Most professional builds land at $3,000–$15,000 ([Jim][jim], citing OneLittleWeb's 2026 study). A quote under $1,000 usually means a template with your content dropped in.

## What pushes the price up or down

Price follows hours, and the hours come from five things: unique layouts, features, integrations, content and deadlines. The hourly rate matters less. The biggest savings come from having your content ready and launching with fewer features.

| What drives the cost | Why it adds cost | How to keep it down |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Custom design | Each unique page layout is designed, built and tested separately | Reuse a few layouts across pages, and start from a proven theme |
| Features | Booking, payments, logins and search each add design, build and testing time | Launch with what you need now, and add the rest in a second phase |
| Integrations | Connecting a CRM, booking tool, accounting or inventory system is the most common reason "simple" projects double in cost ([Ecosire][ecosire]) | Use ready-made connectors before custom code, and list every system up front |
| User roles (web apps) | Each extra role, such as client, staff or admin, adds about 15–25% to build hours ([TekRevol][tekrevol]) | Start with one or two roles |
| Content | Copywriting and photography run about $50–$150 per page ([Digitaleer][digitaleer]); content can be 20–30% of a budget ([Lean Labs][leanlabs]) | Provide finished text, your logo and photos before work starts |
| Products and migration | Product photos run $25–$100 each; moving a store from another platform runs $1,500–$15,000 ([Ecosire][ecosire]) | Import from a clean spreadsheet, and launch with your best sellers first |
| Deadlines | A fixed, tight date means overtime and less room for feedback | Give a flexible window, and answer feedback within two business days |
| Accessibility and compliance | Testing to accessibility standards (WCAG) and handling privacy rules take extra time | Build it in from the start: fixing it later costs far more ([Dev.co][devco]) |

Two more things to check in any quote:

- **Revision rounds.** Quotes usually include a set number of rounds, and extra rounds are billed by the hour. One decision-maker giving combined feedback saves money.
- **Hosting.** A plain informational site can run on free or cheap static hosting. A store or web app needs paid hosting and more upkeep.

## What to budget after launch

A typical small business website costs $50–$500 a month to keep running ([GoDaddy][godaddy]). Where yours lands depends on the platform, and on whether someone else does the updates.

| Item | Typical cost | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Domain name (.com) | $10–$20+ a year ([GoDaddy][godaddy]) | The wholesale price of a .com rose to $10.97 on November 1, 2026, so no registrar can charge less than $11.17 ([TLDES][tldes]). Cheap first years often renew at $20 or more. |
| Business email | $6–$12 per person a month ([Ecosire][ecosire]) | Google Workspace or Microsoft 365 |
| Hosting for a simple site | $0–$20 a month | Vercel's Hobby plan is free; its Pro plan, aimed at businesses, is $20 a month ([Vercel][vercel]) |
| WordPress hosting | Shared $5–$15; managed $20–$150+ a month ([GoDaddy][godaddy]) | Managed hosting adds backups, a test copy of the site and faster support |
| Website builder plan | Wix $17.77–$159.77 a month, billed yearly ([Wix][wix-plans]); Squarespace $19–$99 a month billed yearly, or $25–$139 billed monthly ([Squarespace][squarespace]) | Hosting is included. Squarespace's Basic plan adds a 2% fee on store sales. |
| Shopify plan | Basic $39, Grow $105, Advanced $399 a month; $29, $79 and $299 billed yearly ([Shopify][shopify]) | Card payments cost 2.9% + 30¢ on Basic; using another payment provider adds 2% |
| Shopify apps | $200–$500 a month across 6–12 apps for an established store ([Ecosire][ecosire]) | The most underestimated running cost |
| Security certificate (SSL) | Free on most hosts; $10–$100 a year for a standard paid one ([GoDaddy][godaddy]) | Small sites rarely need a paid certificate |
| Web app upkeep | 15–25% of the build cost a year ([TekRevol][tekrevol]) | Covers hosting, security fixes and updates |

### Care and support plans

If someone else looks after the site, care plans usually fall into four tiers ([FatLab][fatlab]):

| Tier | Monthly | Typically includes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Budget | $25–$50 | Automated updates, one daily backup, email support within 24–48 hours, no development time |
| Professional | $75–$150 | Updates tested before going live, off-site backups, same-day developer support, emergencies covered |
| Business | $200–$400 | All of the above, plus 2–4 hours of changes a month and a response within 2–4 hours |
| Enterprise | $500–$600+ | 6–10+ hours of changes a month, a response within 1–2 hours, and a dedicated contact |

A brochure site usually needs $75–$150 a month, a site with custom features $150–$250, and a small store $150–$400 ([FatLab][fatlab]).

Watch for hidden fees: emergency calls at $150–$300+ each, malware cleanup at $200–$500+, and backup storage billed separately. A $50 plan can end up costing $200 or more ([FatLab][fatlab]). In Maryland, web hosting is also subject to the 3% sales tax ([Comptroller of Maryland][md-tax]).

## How long it takes

A professional small business site takes 4–8 weeks, a Shopify store 2–12 weeks, and a custom web app two months or more. How quickly you supply content and give feedback decides where a project lands in the range, more than the builder does.

| Project | Do it yourself | Professional build |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Simple brochure site, 1–5 pages | 1–3 days | 1–2 weeks ([Elementor][elementor]) to 2–5 weeks ([Ambrite][ambrite]) |
| Small business site, 5–15 pages | 1–2 weeks | 4–8 weeks ([Elementor][elementor]) |
| Fully custom-designed site | Not practical | 8–12+ weeks ([Ambrite][ambrite]) |
| Shopify store | 3–6 weeks for under 50 products ([Elementor][elementor]) | Freelancer 2–4 weeks; agency with custom design and migration 6–12 weeks; integration-heavy 3–4 months ([Ecosire][ecosire]) |
| Site with booking, memberships or a client portal | Not practical | 10–20+ weeks ([Ambrite][ambrite]) |
| Custom web app | Not practical | Simple first version 2–4 months; mid-complexity 4–7 months; complex 7–12 months ([TekRevol][tekrevol]) |

What slows projects down:

- **Missing content,** the top cause of stalled projects ([Elementor][elementor]). Gathering and writing content takes 1–4 weeks for a small site ([Ambrite][ambrite]).
- **A growing scope,** such as adding booking, a blog or a portal halfway through.
- **Too many approvers,** and slow feedback on designs.
- **Third-party approvals:** payment processors, booking systems and other services can take days to approve an account.
- **Problems with the old site:** redesigns often uncover broken links, outdated plugins or hosting issues first ([Ambrite][ambrite]).

## When Wix or Squarespace is enough, and when it isn't

A website builder is enough for a simple brochure or portfolio site whose owner wants to edit it themselves. A business outgrows it when the site has to connect to other systems, do custom work or compete hard in search. Know one thing before you start: a builder site can't be moved, so leaving means a rebuild.

**A builder is enough when:**

- The site is a brochure, portfolio or menu built from standard template blocks, and the features are unlikely to change ([eSEOspace][eseospace]).
- Search isn't your main source of customers, or you're a local business and the built-in basics (page titles, sitemaps, security certificates) are enough ([eSEOspace][eseospace]).
- The budget is small. Plans start at $17.77 a month on Wix ([Wix][wix-plans]) and $19 on Squarespace, billed yearly ([Squarespace][squarespace]), with hosting and security updates included and no build fee.
- You want to make edits without paying anyone, and you have time to set it up and keep it current.
- You sell a modest catalog. Squarespace charges no store fee from its Core plan up; its Basic plan takes 2% ([Squarespace][squarespace]).

**It isn't enough when:**

- The site has to connect directly to a CRM, inventory, booking or accounting system ([eSEOspace][eseospace]).
- You need custom logic: quote or booking flows, customer portals, calculators, dashboards or dynamic pricing ([eSEOspace][eseospace]).
- You're paying for several add-ons to force features the platform wasn't built for. This is the clearest sign ([eSEOspace][eseospace]); a $20 plan can grow to hundreds a month ([Harmonic Design][harmonic]).
- You compete for hard search terms and need control over page structure, structured data and speed ([eSEOspace][eseospace]).
- Accessibility problems sit in the platform's own code. Overlay widgets don't fix structural issues ([Harmonic Design][harmonic]).
- You need to be able to leave. Wix says a site must stay on its servers and can't move to another host ([Wix][wix-export]). Squarespace exports pages and posts as content only, without the design ([Stacktree][stacktree]).

**The middle ground:** WordPress or Webflow, built by a professional, gives you an editor like a builder's with more room to grow. A WordPress site can be exported and hosted anywhere; a Webflow site can't leave whole ([Project Cost Estimator][pce-webflow]).

## Red flags when you're hiring someone

The costliest red flags are about control: who owns the domain, the logins and the files. Most of the rest show up before you sign, in how a provider quotes and what the contract leaves out.

| Red flag | What a good provider does instead |
| --- | --- |
| Registers the domain in their own name | Registers it in your name and account, so you control the address and email ([UENI][ueni]) |
| No admin or editing login for you | Hands over the logins and shows you how to make simple edits ([UENI][ueni]) |
| Every small change billed separately | States edit costs up front, or includes editing hours in a care plan ([UENI][ueni]) |
| A contract that says nothing about who owns the design, code and content | Puts ownership, scope, payments, timeline, change handling and support in writing ([WRTeam][wrteam]) |
| A price before any questions | Asks about your business first, gives a ballpark early and a firm quote after scoping ([WRTeam][wrteam]) |
| A very low price with no explanation | Explains what the price covers and what it assumes ([WRTeam][wrteam]) |
| Wants everything paid up front, or refuses staged payments | Takes a deposit, then payments tied to defined milestones ([WRTeam][wrteam]) |
| A portfolio with no live links or references you can reach | Shares case studies with live sites, and clients who will talk to you ([WRTeam][wrteam]) |
| Pressure to sign quickly | Gives you time to compare, and welcomes questions ([WRTeam][wrteam]) |
| No plan or price for after launch | Defines support scope, response times and monthly costs before you sign ([WRTeam][wrteam]) |
| A cheap care plan with extra fees for emergencies and malware cleanup | Lists what's included, with emergencies covered rather than billed at $150–$300 each ([FatLab][fatlab]) |
| Promises a #1 ranking on Google | Explains the search work plainly. Google says no one can guarantee a #1 ranking ([Google][google]) |
| Sells an accessibility overlay widget as the fix | Fixes the site's structure, because overlays don't fix structural issues ([Harmonic Design][harmonic]) |
| Builds on a platform only they can host, with no exit plan | Tells you what it costs to leave. Moving from Webflow to WordPress runs about $2,500–$8,000 ([Project Cost Estimator][pce-webflow]) |

Google also warns against firms that email you out of the blue, or that are secretive about what they will do ([Google][google]).

## How web projects are usually priced

Most small business web work is sold as a fixed price against a written scope, paid in stages, with hourly or monthly billing for anything after launch. Projects with a lot of unknowns usually start with a paid discovery phase.

| Pricing model | When it's used | What to watch for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fixed project price | A clearly defined scope: a set number of pages and features ([Agiled][agiled]) | Extra revisions and late content are usually billed as changes |
| Phased or hourly | Real unknowns, such as integrations, migrations or an undecided scope; it switches to a fixed price once the scope is known ([Agiled][agiled]) | An open-ended bill, so ask for a cap or an estimate |
| Value-based | Stores and lead-generating pages, priced on the revenue the site should bring in ([Agiled][agiled]) | You'll be asked for revenue figures |
| Monthly retainer or care plan | Ongoing updates, edits and improvements ([Agiled][agiled]) | The scope drifts without a cap on hours |

- **Payment schedule:** usually a 40–50% deposit before work starts, a payment when you sign off the design, and the final payment before launch or handover ([Agiled][agiled]).
- **The scope document:** it should list the pages, who supplies the content, the number of revision rounds (two is common), the features and what's excluded. Extra pages and revisions become change orders, and a quote with no list of exclusions invites scope creep ([Agiled][agiled]).
- **The rate behind a fixed price:** fixed project prices work out to roughly $75–$200 an hour of professional time ([Agiled][agiled]).
