**The short answer:** a federal buyer can spend up to $15,000 on a purchase card with no competing quotes, and purchases above that, up to $350,000, are generally reserved for small businesses ([FAR 2.101][far-2-101]; [FAR subpart 13.2][far-13-2]; [FAR 19.502-2][far-19-502-2]). A Maryland agency can buy up to $100,000 on its own authority without having to post the purchase, and must reserve nearly every purchase for certified small businesses when at least two can do the work ([Maryland Procurement Manual][mpm-3]; [Office of State Procurement][osp-memo]).

The route depends on three things: the dollar value, whether at least two capable small firms can compete, and whether you buy directly or through a prime contractor. This guide covers the federal thresholds and set-aside rules, what a firm's SAM.gov registration tells you, Maryland's small procurement categories and Small Business Reserve, how prime contractors find and use small subcontractors, and how to check a firm before you buy from it or team with it.

This guide is general information, not contracting advice. Check with your contracting office before you rely on it, and because thresholds and program rules change, confirm current figures with the official source: acquisition.gov, sam.gov, sba.gov or the Maryland Procurement Manual.

## At a glance

| Buying route | Dollar range | How the buy happens | What the firm needs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Federal micro-purchase | Up to $15,000 ([FAR 2.101][far-2-101]) | Usually a government purchase card, with no competing quotes required ([FAR subpart 13.2][far-13-2]) | To be known to you and able to take a card |
| Federal simplified purchase | Above $15,000, up to $350,000 ([FAR 2.101][far-2-101]) | Generally reserved for small businesses when two or more can compete ([FAR 19.502-2][far-19-502-2]) | SAM.gov registration, and small under the size standard for its industry code |
| Federal set-aside or program award | Above $350,000 | Set aside when two or more small businesses are expected to offer at fair market prices ([FAR 19.502-2][far-19-502-2]) | Registration, plus a certification for program awards such as 8(a) |
| Federal subcontract | Any size; prime contracts over $900,000 need a small business subcontracting plan ([FAR 19.702][far-19-702]) | The prime contractor chooses | A capability statement, registration, and whatever the prime contract passes down |
| Maryland small procurement, Category I | Up to $5,000 ([Maryland Procurement Manual][mpm-3]) | A buyer or purchasing-card holder, after trying for two quotes | An eMMA profile and small business certification |
| Maryland small procurement, Category II | Above $5,000, up to $100,000 ([Maryland Procurement Manual][mpm-3]) | Two or three quotes; posting on eMMA is optional | The same |
| Maryland competitive procurement | Above $100,000 | Posted on eMMA | The same, plus a full bid or proposal |

The federal figures come from the Federal Acquisition Regulation (FAR) in the version effective March 13, 2026, under Federal Acquisition Circular 2026-01 ([FAR 2.101][far-2-101]). The Maryland figures come from the Maryland Procurement Manual, last changed November 13, 2026 ([Maryland Procurement Manual][mpm-3]).

**Older articles quote old limits.** The federal micro-purchase threshold is $15,000, not $10,000, and the simplified acquisition threshold is $350,000, not $250,000 ([FAR 2.101][far-2-101]). Maryland's Category II has run to $100,000, not $50,000, since August 31, 2026 ([Office of State Procurement][osp-aug]).

## Federal purchases up to $350,000

Below $350,000 there are two federal bands, and both make it simple to buy from a small IT firm.

### The thresholds

All four are defined in FAR 2.101 ([FAR 2.101][far-2-101]):

| Threshold | Amount | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Micro-purchase threshold | $15,000 | The general figure |
| Micro-purchase, services under the Service Contract Labor Standards | $2,500 | Whether an IT service is covered depends on the work |
| Micro-purchase, emergencies and contingency operations | $25,000 inside the US; $40,000 outside | Set by the agency head |
| Simplified acquisition threshold | $350,000 | $1 million inside the US and $2 million outside, for emergencies and contingencies |

### Micro-purchases, up to $15,000

Under FAR subpart 13.2 ([FAR subpart 13.2][far-13-2]):

- **Payment:** the government purchase card is the preferred way to buy and pay.
- **Quotes:** none are required if you consider the price reasonable.
- **Set-asides:** there are none at this level. Instead, you're asked to spread micro-purchases fairly among qualified suppliers, as far as practical.

A small website, an accessibility audit, a short training session or a software subscription can fit under $15,000. For typical build prices, see [what a small business website costs](/guides/small-business-website-cost). Check the $2,500 limit first if the service falls under the Service Contract Labor Standards ([FAR 2.101][far-2-101]).

### Purchases above $15,000, up to $350,000

Under FAR 19.502-2 ([FAR 19.502-2][far-19-502-2]):

- **The default:** the purchase is reserved for small businesses.
- **The exception:** the contracting officer may skip the set-aside when there's no reasonable expectation of offers from at least two responsible small businesses that are competitive on price, quality and delivery.
- **A single offer:** if only one acceptable small business offer arrives, the officer should award to that firm.

Simplified procedures apply in this range, which usually means a shorter solicitation and a faster award than a full competition. A larger build or a year of support can fit here, with the competition among small firms. Treat one requirement as one purchase, and don't split it into several small buys to stay under a threshold.

## Federal set-asides and certified programs

A set-aside limits a competition to small businesses. Four programs go further and limit it to a certified group.

### The rule of two

| Purchase size | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Above $15,000, up to $350,000 | Generally reserved for small businesses ([FAR 19.502-2][far-19-502-2]) |
| Above $350,000 | Must be set aside when the contracting officer reasonably expects offers from at least two responsible small businesses at fair market prices ([FAR 19.502-2][far-19-502-2]) |
| Above $350,000, order of consideration | The four certified programs first, then a general small business set-aside; set-asides come before open competition ([FAR part 19][far-19]) |

The two-offer test is known as the rule of two. It's why market research matters: if yours turns up two capable small firms, the work has to be set aside.

### The four certified programs

- **8(a) Business Development:** small businesses owned by socially and economically disadvantaged people.
- **HUBZone:** small businesses based in, and hiring from, designated low-income areas.
- **Women-Owned Small Business:** small businesses owned and controlled by women, in eligible industries.
- **Service-Disabled Veteran-Owned Small Business:** small businesses owned and controlled by service-disabled veterans.

These are short summaries, so check each program's eligibility rules, and how a firm qualifies, at sba.gov. There's no ranking among the four ([FAR 19.203][far-19]). An 8(a) award can be made without competition up to $5.5 million, or $8.5 million for manufacturing; above that, it's generally competed among 8(a) firms ([FAR 19.805-1][far-19-805-1]).

### Size standards and industry codes

- **Industry codes:** these are NAICS codes, from the North American Industry Classification System. A firm lists one primary code, plus others for its other services ([SBA][sba-basics]).
- **The test:** a firm is small if it and its affiliates fall under the SBA's limit for its industry code, with receipts averaged over the latest five fiscal years ([SBA][sba-basics]).
- **The rule of thumb:** average annual receipts under $7.5 million for non-manufacturers, with exceptions by industry ([SBA][sba-basics]).
- **Computer services:** the limit is higher. The SBA's current table of size standards took effect March 17, 2023, so check the exact figure for the code in that table ([SBA][sba-size]).
- **Other conditions:** the firm must also be for-profit, independently owned and operated, not dominant in its field nationally, and operating in the US ([SBA][sba-basics]).

### A small prime has to do part of the work

On a set-aside contract, the small business must perform a minimum share of the work itself. The limits are in the Code of Federal Regulations at 13 CFR 125.6 ([SBA][sba-subcontracting]). A small firm can't simply pass the work to a large one, so check how the work is divided before award.

## What a firm's SAM.gov registration tells you

SAM.gov, the System for Award Management, is where a business registers to do business with the federal government ([SAM.gov][sam]). Contracting officers use its profiles to find firms, and the profile is where a firm states that it is small and claims eligibility for programs ([SBA][sba-basics]).

| Item | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Unique Entity ID (UEI) | A 12-character ID assigned during registration. It replaced the DUNS number, so no outside company is involved ([SBA][sba-basics]) |
| Industry codes (NAICS) | One primary code and others for other services; the size standard follows the code ([SBA][sba-basics]) |
| Renewal | Every 365 days, or the registration goes inactive ([SAM.gov][sam]) |
| Sign-in | A SAM.gov account, through Login.gov ([SAM.gov][sam]) |
| Cost | Free to register and renew, according to guides that cite the government's own statements ([The Bid Lab][bidlab]; [SLED AI][sledai]) |
| Time to become active | Up to 10 business days, SAM.gov's figure as quoted by guides ([SLED AI][sledai]); name or tax ID mismatches can add weeks ([OpenGrants][opengrants]) |
| An ID without full registration | Possible for some purposes, such as being reported as a subcontractor ([SBA][sba-basics]) |

For Defense work, a firm also needs the cybersecurity certification level (CMMC) named in each solicitation, and the ability to invoice electronically ([SBA][sba-basics]).

If you want to use a firm that isn't registered yet, ask it to start early. The most common stall is a legal name or address that doesn't match IRS records exactly ([OpenGrants][opengrants]). Point the firm to sam.gov directly. Registration is free ([The Bid Lab][bidlab]), so a request for a registration fee comes from a third party, not the government.

## Maryland: eMMA and small business certification

Maryland's procurement system is eMMA, short for eMaryland Marketplace Advantage. A vendor profile there is free, and the State's small business certification is applied for inside eMMA. From the certification program's page ([Maryland Department of Social and Economic Mobility][md-csb]):

| Item | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Certified Small Business | The certification that makes a firm eligible for contracts reserved under the Small Business Reserve program |
| How a firm applies | Inside eMMA, after creating its profile |
| Cost | Free to certify and to recertify |
| Time | Approval can take as little as one day |
| How long it lasts | Three years, then renewed in eMMA |
| Who runs it | The Office of Small, Minority & Women Business Affairs, now part of the Maryland Department of Social and Economic Mobility |

## Maryland small procurements, up to $100,000

Every Maryland state agency can buy up to $100,000 on its own authority through a small procurement, and most of that range doesn't have to be advertised ([Maryland Procurement Manual][mpm-3]). You can buy from firms you find in eMMA's small business list.

### The categories

Section 3.4.1 of the Procurement Manual sets four categories ([Maryland Procurement Manual][mpm-3]):

| Category | Dollar range | Quotes needed | Posting on eMMA |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I | $1–$5,000 | An attempt at two, spoken or written | Not required |
| II, lower part | Above $5,000, up to $50,000 | At least two responsive bids or offers | Optional |
| II, upper part | Above $50,000, up to $100,000 | At least two if posted on eMMA; otherwise at least three written quotes | Optional; if posted, for at least three business days |
| III | Above $100,000, up to $200,000 | At least two responsive bids or offers | Required, for at least three business days |

- **Category III isn't for IT.** It covers only certain Department of Natural Resources projects and state construction. For everything else, the small procurement ceiling is $100,000 ([Maryland Procurement Manual][mpm-3]).
- **Category I by card:** an authorized cardholder can make these buys on a state purchasing card ([Maryland Procurement Manual][mpm-3]).
- **No splitting:** a procurement may not be artificially divided to fit a smaller category ([COMAR 21.05.07.05][comar]).

### What changed on August 31, 2026

| Change | Before | Now |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Top of Category II | $50,000 | $100,000 ([Office of State Procurement][osp-aug]) |
| eMMA posting of solicitations and awards | Required above $50,000 ([Board of Public Works][bpw-2004-2]) | Required above $100,000 ([Office of State Procurement][osp-aug]) |

The change was proposed on April 17, 2026, adopted by the Board of Public Works on August 5, 2026, and took effect on August 31, 2026 ([Office of State Procurement][osp-aug]). Some state pages still show the old figures. One section of the Procurement Manual still describes Category II as running to $50,000 ([Maryland Procurement Manual, section 7][mpm-7]).

### Above $100,000

- **Posting:** solicitations and awards are posted on eMMA ([Office of State Procurement][osp-aug]).
- **Review:** purchases above the small procurement level are reviewed by the Office of State Procurement ([Office of State Procurement][osp-aug-followup]).

**A fee to know about.** The Board of Public Works approved a 1% administrative fee, paid by contractors, on procurements controlled by the Department of General Services. The briefing that describes it gives no start date and no exemptions ([Office of State Procurement][osp-feb]). Expect bidders to ask whether it applies to your purchase.

## Maryland's Small Business Reserve

Under a June 17, 2026 policy memo, Maryland agencies must reserve nearly every purchase for certified small businesses unless fewer than two can do the work ([Office of State Procurement][osp-memo]). On a reserved solicitation, only Certified Small Businesses can compete, and they bid as the prime contractor ([Maryland Department of Social and Economic Mobility][md-csb]).

### How the program works

From the program's page ([Maryland Department of Social and Economic Mobility][md-csb]):

| Item | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Started | 2004 |
| Goal | Each agency works to award at least 20% of its procurement dollars directly to small businesses |
| Price preference on other eligible solicitations | Up to 5%; 7% if veteran-owned; 8% if disabled-veteran-owned |
| Eligibility | For-profit, independently owned and operated, not dominant in its field, and under the size limit for its industry |

The page doesn't list the size limits, so confirm them with the Office of Small, Minority & Women Business Affairs.

### When a purchase must be reserved

The June 17, 2026 memo sets the rule by purchase size ([Office of State Procurement][osp-memo]):

| Purchase size | Reserve for small business? | Minority and veteran goals |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $50,000 or less | Required, unless fewer than two certified small businesses can do the work, or it's impracticable | Not required |
| Above $50,000, up to $100,000 | Required, unless fewer than two can do the work | Formal goal-setting not required |
| Above $100,000, up to $1 million | Required, with the same exception | Market research and goal assessment required |
| Above $1 million | Directed by Executive Order 01.01.2024.38, with the same exception or where impracticable | Market research and goal assessment required |

- **It reaches card buys:** since October 1, 2025, the reserve applies to all small procurement categories, including purchasing-card buys ([Maryland Procurement Manual][mpm-3]).
- **Document it:** record any decision not to reserve. Above $50,000, exemptions and waivers go to the Office of Small, Minority & Women Business Affairs ([Office of State Procurement][osp-memo]).
- **Finding firms:** search eMMA for vendors marked as Small Business Reserve ([Maryland Procurement Manual][mpm-3-excerpt]).

### Minority and veteran programs

- **They work differently.** The reserve decides who can be the prime. The Minority Business Enterprise (MBE) and Veteran-Owned Small Business Enterprise (VSBE) programs mostly set subcontracting goals on larger contracts ([Office of State Procurement][osp-memo]).
- **They can stack.** Above $100,000, a reserved procurement may also carry minority or veteran subcontracting goals where subcontracting is possible ([Office of State Procurement][osp-memo]).
- **Small buys:** contact certified minority and veteran firms along with other vendors ([Maryland Procurement Manual][mpm-3-excerpt]).

### Paying on time

The State must pay within 30 days of the later of the due date and receipt of a proper invoice, with interest if it pays late. On a Small Business Reserve contract, the deadline is 15 days ([State Finance and Procurement § 15-103][md-15-103]).

## How prime contractors find and use small subcontractors

A large federal contractor whose contract is expected to exceed $900,000 must have a plan for subcontracting to small businesses ([FAR 19.702][far-19-702]). That plan is why primes look for small firms, and the prime, not the government, chooses who gets the work.

### When a subcontracting plan is required

| Item | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| When a plan is required | Contracts expected to exceed $900,000, or $2 million for construction, that have subcontracting possibilities ([FAR 19.702][far-19-702]) |
| Who's exempt | Small businesses holding the prime contract; personal services contracts; work done entirely outside the US ([FAR 19.702][far-19-702]) |
| What the plan contains | Goals for awards to categories of small business ([SBA][sba-subcontracting]) |
| Who checks it | SBA staff help with plans before award and with compliance after ([SBA][sba-subcontracting]) |

### Where to find small firms

The SBA points to these tools ([SBA][sba-subcontracting]):

- **SUBNet:** the SBA's board where large businesses post subcontracting opportunities.
- **Small Business Search:** the SBA's database for finding small firms. The more complete a firm's profile, the more you can screen from it.

### What to ask a small subcontractor for

This is common practice rather than a rule from a single source.

| Ask for | Why |
| --- | --- |
| A one- or two-page capability statement | A quick screen of services, industry codes, certifications, past work and contacts |
| Unique Entity ID and SAM.gov status | You report your subcontract awards |
| Certifications | Awards to certified firms count toward your plan's goals |
| Past performance, including commercial work | Evidence the firm delivers |
| Insurance certificates | Often general liability, professional liability and cyber cover |
| Rates or a fixed price | To build your own bid |
| Security requirements met | Background checks or clearances; a cybersecurity certification level on Defense work |
| A signed confidentiality or teaming agreement | Before you share the solicitation details |

### What flows down to the subcontractor

- **Contract clauses:** your obligations on security, data handling, accessibility and labor rules flow into the subcontract. Share them before the firm prices the work.
- **Payment timing:** when the government pays you early under an accelerated payment, you must pay your small business subcontractors within 15 days of receiving it, once a proper invoice is in, and you can't charge them a fee for it ([FAR 52.232-40][far-52-232-40]). Otherwise payment follows the subcontract's own terms.
- **Maryland's 3% tax:** a subcontractor selling taxable IT services to you must charge the tax, even when your customer is a government. You can't use the government's exemption, and a contract clause can't change that. Sales made directly to the federal or state government are exempt ([Comptroller of Maryland][md-tax]). For more, see [Maryland's 3% tax on IT and software services](/guides/maryland-tax-on-it-services).

### Line up the team early

Primes usually form their teams before a solicitation comes out, often months ahead. A small firm you meet after the bid is public usually comes too late for that bid. A narrow, specific role, such as accessibility testing or work on a named platform, is easier to define, price and place than general IT support.

## How to check a small IT firm

Most checks take minutes: an active registration, a status the firm can support, and terms that put the work in writing.

| Check | What to look for |
| --- | --- |
| SAM.gov registration | Active, since it must be renewed every 365 days ([SAM.gov][sam]) |
| Small business status | Size and ownership facts that support what the firm states in its SAM.gov profile ([SBA][sba-basics]) |
| Program certifications | The certification or self-certification behind any 8(a), HUBZone, women-owned or service-disabled veteran-owned claim |
| Maryland certification | Marked as Small Business Reserve in eMMA ([Maryland Procurement Manual][mpm-3-excerpt]), with a certification that lasts three years ([Maryland Department of Social and Economic Mobility][md-csb]) |
| Work share on a set-aside | The small firm does at least the minimum share itself under 13 CFR 125.6 ([SBA][sba-subcontracting]) |
| Teaming agreement | Signed, and stating the work and what happens on award |
| Subcontract payment terms | A fixed number of days, not "paid when paid" with no time limit |
| Maryland pricing | Quotes that include the 3% tax where it applies ([Comptroller of Maryland][md-tax]) and say whether the 1% state fee is included ([Office of State Procurement][osp-feb]) |

Warning signs:

- **A suggestion to split an order** to stay under a limit. Maryland's regulation forbids artificial division ([COMAR 21.05.07.05][comar]).
- **Old thresholds** in a proposal or teaming pitch, such as a $10,000 micro-purchase limit or a $50,000 top for Maryland's Category II.
- **A claimed status the firm can't back up** with size figures, ownership facts or a certification.

## How long it takes

Small purchases are fast. A firm's registration takes days to weeks, so ask early if a firm you want isn't registered or certified.

| Step | Typical time |
| --- | --- |
| Federal micro-purchase | Can be the same day once you decide, since it's a card purchase with no quotes required |
| Maryland Category I or II purchase | Days to a few weeks, with two or three quotes and no posting required |
| Maryland posted solicitation | Open at least three business days; larger ones much longer ([Maryland Procurement Manual][mpm-3]) |
| A firm's SAM.gov registration | Up to 10 business days to become active, SAM.gov's figure as quoted by guides ([SLED AI][sledai]); 2–6 weeks if records don't match, in one contractor's account ([Buildermuse][buildermuse]) |
| A firm's Maryland small business certification | As little as one day ([Maryland Department of Social and Economic Mobility][md-csb]) |
