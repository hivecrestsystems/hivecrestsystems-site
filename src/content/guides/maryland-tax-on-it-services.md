**The short answer:** since July 1, 2025, Maryland has charged a 3% sales tax on most IT, data and software services, including custom websites, custom programming, web hosting, cloud storage and IT consulting ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]). You pay it as the buyer. The seller collects it, shows it as its own line on the invoice and sends it to the state ([Technical Bulletin 54][tb54]; [Tax-General Article § 11-302][md-11-302]).

Whether a purchase is taxed depends on the service itself, not on who sells it. Software subscriptions, exempt buyers and contracts signed before the tax began each follow their own rules. This guide covers what is taxed, how the tax appears on quotes and invoices, the exemptions, and what has changed since 2025, so you can check an invoice and budget for the tax.

This guide is general information, not tax advice. Check how the tax applies to your purchases with a tax professional or the Comptroller of Maryland, and because the rules change, confirm current details in the Comptroller's own publications.

## At a glance

| Question | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| What is the rate? | 3% of the price of the service ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]) |
| When did it start? | July 1, 2025 ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]) |
| What law created it? | Chapter 604 of the Acts of 2025 ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]), from House Bill 352, the state's budget reconciliation act ([Lookuptax][lookuptax]) |
| What is taxed? | Data processing and hosting, information services, computer systems design and custom programming, and software publishing ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]) |
| Who pays? | The buyer ([Technical Bulletin 54][tb54]) |
| Who collects? | The seller, which needs a Maryland sales and use tax license ([Comptroller, 2025 tax changes][md-2025]) |
| Software by subscription? | 3% when a business buys it for its own systems; 6% when a person buys it for individual use ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]) |
| Small business exemption? | None. The exemptions the Comptroller lists don't depend on the size of the buyer or the seller ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]) |
| Still in force? | Yes. Efforts to repeal it failed in the 2026 legislative session ([Taxually][taxually]) |

**Two errors to watch for.** The rate is 3% or 6%, never the two added together as 9% ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]). And sales between affiliated companies have been exempt since July 1, 2026 ([Tax Alert 2026–2027][alert-2026]), even though the Comptroller's main bulletin, last updated June 30, 2025, still says they are taxed ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]).

## What the tax covers

The tax covers services in four categories of the North American Industry Classification System (NAICS), the federal system of industry codes: 518, 519, 5415 and 5132 ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]). What counts is the service you buy, not the seller's own industry code. A business classified under another code that sells one of these services must still charge the tax, and each service is judged on its own ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]).

| Category | What it means | Examples from the Comptroller's list |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 518: data processing and hosting | Running or renting computing power and storage | Web hosting, application hosting, cloud storage and cloud computing, infrastructure and platform services, data entry, data processing, optical scanning, server space in data centers |
| 519: information services | Supplying or storing information | Internet search portals, archives and libraries, news and press clipping services, stock photos |
| 5415: computer systems design | Designing, building and running systems for a client | Custom webpage design, custom programming and software development, custom software support, software installation, hardware and software consulting, network and systems integration, managing a client's computer systems, disaster recovery, custom search engine optimization, design of software that automates routine tasks (robotic process automation) |
| 5132: software publishing | Selling ready-made software | Packaged software, operating systems, non-custom applications and mobile apps, game software |

The full list is Section IV of Technical Bulletin 56 ([Technical Bulletin 56, PDF][tb56-pdf]). The Comptroller also submitted it for the Code of Maryland Regulations (COMAR) as regulation 03.06.01.48 ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]).

### What a small business typically buys

| Purchase | Taxed at 3%? | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A custom website | Yes | Custom webpage design is on the list ([Technical Bulletin 56, PDF][tb56-pdf]) |
| A custom app or software | Yes | Custom programming is on the list, and the old exemption for customized software ended on July 1, 2025 ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]) |
| Web hosting or cloud storage | Yes | Both are listed under 518 ([Technical Bulletin 56, PDF][tb56-pdf]) |
| IT consulting, software installation, network setup | Yes | All are listed under 5415 ([Technical Bulletin 56, PDF][tb56-pdf]) |
| Outsourced IT management and software support | Likely | Managing a client's computer systems and custom software support are listed, so check each service ([Technical Bulletin 56, PDF][tb56-pdf]) |
| Search engine optimization (SEO) | Yes, if custom | Custom SEO is listed, except hosting and infrastructure support ([Technical Bulletin 56, PDF][tb56-pdf]) |
| Business software by subscription | Yes, at 3% | See [software subscriptions](#software-subscriptions-3-or-6) ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]) |
| Payroll processing | No | Payroll and financial transaction processing are carved out of data processing ([Technical Bulletin 56, PDF][tb56-pdf]) |
| Computers and equipment | Not this tax | They are goods, which carry Maryland's general 6% sales tax ([Taxually][taxually]) |
| Logo design, advertising, staff training | Not on the list | The list doesn't name them and the bulletin doesn't rule on them ([Technical Bulletin 56, PDF][tb56-pdf]), so confirm before you rely on it |

### Two points that surprise people

- **A contract can't switch it off.** If a contract bars the seller from charging the tax, the seller still owes it to the state ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]). Expect sellers to build it into their prices.
- **Hiring from a neighboring state isn't a way around it.** Virginia and Pennsylvania impose no similar tax ([Withum][withum]). But a seller outside Maryland must collect Maryland tax once it passes $100,000 in sales or 200 separate transactions delivered into Maryland in a calendar year ([Comptroller tax alert, 2019][sut-2019]). If a seller doesn't charge the tax on a taxable service, you generally owe it yourself as use tax ([Zamp][zamp]).

## Software subscriptions: 3% or 6%

Software you use over the internet by subscription (often called software as a service, or SaaS) is taxed at 3% when a business buys it for its own systems, and at 6% when a person buys it for individual use ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]). The two rates are never added together.

| Purchase | Rate | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Subscription software for a business's computer systems | 3% | It counts as a taxable software publishing service ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]) |
| Subscription software for individual use | 6% | It counts as a digital product ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]) |
| A sale that could fall under both | The higher rate | Maryland law says the higher rate applies ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]) |

- **The business test:** software bought only for use in what the bulletin calls an "enterprise computer system" isn't treated as a digital product. That holds whether the system runs on the buyer's own equipment or on a cloud server run by the buyer, the vendor or someone else ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]).
- **An example:** under that rule, an accounting subscription your company uses would carry 3%, and a personal streaming or photo-storage subscription would carry 6%. These examples are an interpretation, not the Comptroller's.
- **9% is wrong.** At least one sales tax website describes a 9% combined rate ([Sales.tax][salestax]), which contradicts the Comptroller's bulletin. If you see 9% on an invoice for software or IT services, question it.
- **Custom software lost its exemption.** Customized or configured software used to have an exemption, and it was repealed on July 1, 2025 ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]). Custom software now falls under the 3% tax as custom programming ([Technical Bulletin 56, PDF][tb56-pdf]).

## Who pays and who collects

The buyer pays the tax and the seller collects it: the Comptroller says a vendor must generally collect the tax from the buyer on these sales ([Technical Bulletin 54][tb54]). Sellers register through Maryland Tax Connect, the Comptroller's online tax system, for a sales and use tax license ([Comptroller, 2025 tax changes][md-2025]), which is free ([Numeral][numeral]). The sales thresholds above apply only to sellers based outside Maryland ([Comptroller tax alert, 2019][sut-2019]).

What this means for you as a buyer:

- **If no tax is charged,** you generally owe it yourself as use tax, the tax a buyer pays directly when the seller didn't collect it. Maryland has a consumer use tax return, due by the 20th of the month after each quarter ([Zamp][zamp]).
- **If you're exempt,** give the seller proof of your exemption before you buy. A qualifying buyer has to present it; the seller can't assume it ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]).
- **Expect the tax in your provider's own costs.** A provider pays the tax on hosting, cloud services and subcontracted programming it uses to deliver your project, because the resale exemption covers only services resold in the same form without being used. The bulletin itself notes that this raises a contractor's cost of doing the work ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]).
- **If your business is outside Maryland:** one summary of the law says these sales are taxed based on the customer's tax address ([Lookuptax][lookuptax]). The Comptroller's bulletins don't spell out where a sale is taxed, so confirm before you assume a Maryland seller shouldn't charge you.

## How the tax shows up on quotes and invoices

The tax appears as its own line, separate from the price of the service. Maryland law requires sales tax to be stated separately from the sale price on any record of the sale ([Tax-General Article § 11-302][md-11-302]). A seller that doesn't pass the tax on to you still has to pay it to the state ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]).

### Example: a single service

| Line | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Custom website design | $5,000.00 |
| Maryland sales tax, 3% | $150.00 |
| Total | $5,150.00 |

### Example: a mixed invoice

| Line | Amount | Tax | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Network setup and software installation | $1,200.00 | $36.00 at 3% | A listed IT service ([Technical Bulletin 56, PDF][tb56-pdf]) |
| Two laptops | $2,400.00 | $144.00 at 6% | Goods carry the general 6% rate ([Taxually][taxually]) |
| Cloud backup, one year | $300.00 | $9.00 at 3% | Cloud storage is listed ([Technical Bulletin 56, PDF][tb56-pdf]) |
| Total | $3,900.00 | $189.00 | $4,089.00 with tax |

These examples are illustrations, not the Comptroller's.

- **Ask for itemized lines,** so the 3% falls only on the taxable services. Each service is judged individually ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]), and the bulletins don't cover one price that bundles taxed and untaxed work.
- **Subscriptions and renewals:** each subscription payment counts as a separate sale, and an automatically renewing contract is treated the same way, so each invoice carries its own tax line ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]).
- **Exempt buyers:** a qualifying nonprofit's invoice shows no tax, and the seller keeps its exemption certificate number for at least four years ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]).
- **Buyers with a multiple points of use certificate:** the seller charges no tax, and you pay tax on the Maryland share yourself ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]). See [services used inside and outside Maryland](#services-used-inside-and-outside-maryland).
- **Quotes:** a quote for taxable services that doesn't mention tax will come in 3% higher on the invoice. Ask "is that before tax?" before you sign. For what the build itself should cost, see [what a small business website really costs](/guides/small-business-website-cost).

## Exemptions and special cases

Exemptions exist for government and qualifying nonprofit buyers, true resale, sales within a corporate group, services used in more than one state, and two narrow technology cases. None applies on its own: in most cases the buyer has to give the seller a certificate or other proof ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]).

| Case | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Federal and state government | Exempt on their own purchases ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]) |
| Contractors working for government | Not exempt. A contractor can't use its government customer's exemption, and pays the tax on services it buys to perform the contract ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]) |
| Nonprofits | Qualifying charitable, religious and educational organizations are exempt when the purchase serves their purpose and they hold a Maryland sales and use tax exemption certificate ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]) |
| Resale | Exempt only if the service is resold in the same form and the buyer hasn't used it. A web host reselling cloud storage qualifies; one using that storage to deliver other services doesn't ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]) |
| Affiliated companies | Exempt since July 1, 2026, when seller and buyer belong to the same affiliated group as federal tax law defines it ([Tax Alert 2026–2027][alert-2026]). Before that date these sales were taxed, even at cost ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]) |
| Qualified cybersecurity businesses | Cloud computing sold to a for-profit firm that mainly develops its own cybersecurity technology or provides cybersecurity services is exempt ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]) |
| University of Maryland Discovery District | Sales to and by qualifying companies there are exempt in specified cases ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]) |

The Comptroller doesn't issue certificates for the cybersecurity or Discovery District exemptions. A buyer claiming one shows the seller proof, and tax paid in error can be refunded by the seller or through Form SUT205, the Comptroller's refund application ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]).

If you hold a government contract, budget for the 3% on the IT services you buy to perform it ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]). For how agencies and prime contractors buy IT work, see [how agencies and primes buy from small IT firms](/guides/how-government-buys-it-services).

### Services used inside and outside Maryland

If you know at the time of purchase that a service will be used both inside and outside Maryland, you can give the seller a multiple points of use (MPU) certificate. The seller then charges no tax, and you pay tax on the Maryland share directly ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]).

| Rule | Now | From January 1, 2027 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who can issue one | A buyer with a Maryland sales and use tax account, authorized by the Comptroller ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]) | The same, with the authorization renewed every two years ([Tax Alert 2026–2027][alert-2026]) |
| How many | Generally one per transaction; one can cover a subscription while its terms and Maryland share stay the same ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]) | One per seller, in effect until you withdraw it ([Tax Alert 2026–2027][alert-2026]) |
| What the seller does | Checks it on Maryland Tax Connect, and refuses it if the service will be used only in Maryland ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]) | Checks it with the Comptroller's verification tool ([Tax Alert 2026–2027][alert-2026]) |
| Splitting the price | Any reasonable method, such as by employees or licenses, applied consistently ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]) | The same ([Tax Alert 2026–2027][alert-2026]) |

The 2027 changes come from Chapters 197 and 198 of 2026 ([Tax Alert 2026–2027][alert-2026]); the current certificate rules are also in [Technical Bulletin 54][tb54]. You can always skip the certificate and pay tax on the full price ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]).

## Contracts and subscriptions that began before July 1, 2025

Work under a fixed contract signed before July 1, 2025 stays untaxed, but subscriptions, renewals and anything added later are taxed. These rules still matter for long projects and multi-year agreements.

| Situation | Taxed? |
| --- | --- |
| Installment or milestone contract signed before July 1, 2025, with payments or work after that date | No ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]) |
| Option years or optional services exercised later under a contract signed before July 1, 2025 | No ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]) |
| Change order adding new services after July 1, 2025 | Yes ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]) |
| Subscription that began before July 1, 2025 | Yes, on each payment from July 1, 2025 ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]) |
| Automatic renewal after July 1, 2025 | Yes ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]) |
| Sale on credit completed before July 1, 2025, paid or delivered later | No ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]) |
| The seller's own purchases after July 1, 2025 to perform an older contract | Yes ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]) |

In practice, a web or software project signed in spring 2025 and still running carries no tax on its original scope. A monthly hosting or support plan from the same period has been taxed on every payment since July 1, 2025. A new phase or added feature is taxed even when the original contract isn't ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]).

## What has changed, and what is still unsettled

Since it began, the tax has gained one new exemption and tighter certificate rules, and it has raised far less than forecast. Anything written before mid-2026 may be out of date.

| Date | What happened |
| --- | --- |
| May 20, 2025 | The governor approved House Bill 352 ([Lookuptax][lookuptax]) |
| June 10 and 30, 2025 | The Comptroller published Technical Bulletin 56, then updated it ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]) |
| July 1, 2025 | The tax took effect under emergency regulations ([Technical Bulletin 54][tb54]) |
| December 12, 2025 | The Comptroller proposed permanent regulations, COMAR 03.06.01.48, .49 and .50, with comments closing January 26, 2026 ([Comptroller press release][press-dec-2025]) |
| March 2, 2026 | Final regulations took effect, including the list of taxed services and the certificate rules ([Bloomberg Tax][bloomberg]) |
| 2026 legislative session | Repeal efforts failed ([Taxually][taxually]) |
| July 1, 2026 | Sales between members of an affiliated group became exempt, under Chapters 351 and 352 of 2026 ([Tax Alert 2026–2027][alert-2026]) |
| About September 2026 | First-year revenue was reported at $112.8 million, about 77% below forecast ([Citybiz][citybiz]) |
| January 1, 2027 | New MPU certificate rules begin, and sales and use tax returns for periods beginning after December 31, 2026 must be filed electronically, with narrow exceptions ([Tax Alert 2026–2027][alert-2026]) |

**The revenue gap.** Legislative analysts estimated the tax would bring in $482.0 million ([Department of Legislative Services][dls]). It brought in $112.8 million in fiscal 2026, paid by about 2,900 companies ([Citybiz][citybiz]). A gap that large keeps the tax under debate, so check for new laws from the 2027 legislative session before you rely on any summary, this one included.

### Still unsettled or easy to get wrong

- **The main bulletin is partly out of date.** Technical Bulletin 56 hasn't been revised since June 30, 2025, and still says sales to affiliated companies are taxed ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]). The 2026 exemption overrides that from July 1, 2026 ([Tax Alert 2026–2027][alert-2026]).
- **Bundled prices.** Neither bulletin says how to tax one price that covers both taxed and untaxed work.
- **Where a sale is taxed.** The bulletins don't define it. The certificate rules cover use in more than one state, but not every case.
- **Services not named on the list,** such as security monitoring, AI tools, training or general help desk work. Each has to be compared with the listed descriptions ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]), and reasonable people can differ. The Comptroller's Legal Division takes questions, and its contact details are at the end of the bulletin ([Technical Bulletin 56, PDF][tb56-pdf]).
- **Local governments.** The bulletin addresses federal and state buyers and their contractors ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]). Counties, cities and school systems should confirm their own exemption status with the Comptroller.

## What to do if you buy IT services in Maryland

- **Budget 3% on taxable services,** and ask whether every quote includes tax before you sign.
- **Tell software vendors when a subscription is for business use,** since that is taxed at 3%, not 6% ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]).
- **Send exemption paperwork before the purchase** if you're a qualifying nonprofit, or a cybersecurity or Discovery District company ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]).
- **Consider an MPU certificate** if a service will be used both in Maryland and elsewhere. You'll need a Maryland sales and use tax account and the Comptroller's authorization ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]).
- **Account for use tax** when a seller doesn't charge Maryland tax on a taxable service ([Zamp][zamp]).
- **Review older contracts.** Change orders, renewals and subscription payments since July 1, 2025 are taxed even when the original contract isn't ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]).

### Red flags on quotes and invoices

| Red flag | Why it matters | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A Maryland IT invoice with no tax line and no explanation | You may owe the 3% yourself as use tax ([Zamp][zamp]) | Ask whether the service is taxable and whether the seller is registered |
| A rate of 9% on software or IT services | The rates are 3% or 6%; the higher one applies, and they aren't added ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]) | Ask the seller to correct it |
| 6% on a business software subscription | Software bought for a business's systems is taxed at 3% ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]) | Tell the seller the purchase is for business use |
| "Tax included" with no separate amount | The tax must be stated separately ([Tax-General Article § 11-302][md-11-302]) | Ask for an itemized invoice |
| A seller that skips the tax because its industry code isn't on the list | Taxability follows the service sold, not the seller's code ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]) | Expect tax on listed services, or owe use tax yourself |
| No tax because you hold a government contract | Only the government's own purchases are exempt ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]) | Budget 3% on IT services you buy to perform the contract |
| An exempt nonprofit charged the tax | Exempt buyers must give the seller their certificate first ([Technical Bulletin 56][tb56]) | Send the certificate and ask the seller to refund tax charged in error |
